# How AtRIS uses Geant4

AtRIS follows energetic particles through a planetary atmosphere and surface.
It was originally developed by Saša Banjac; see
[Banjac, Herbst, and Heber (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1029/2018JA026042).
You supply the planetary model and particle source. Geant4 follows the
particles through that model, and AtRIS records the results.

## What Geant4 and AtRIS each provide

[Geant4](https://geant4.web.cern.ch/) is a toolkit for simulating particles
moving through matter. It follows changes of energy and direction,
interactions, decays, and the secondary particles produced along the way.
The chosen [physics list](physics-lists.md) supplies the processes and models
used in that transport.

AtRIS defines the planet's geometry and materials, the incoming radiation,
and what to record at each atmospheric layer. The
compiled `AtRIS` program uses Geant4 as a library. The accompanying Python
tools prepare its inputs and process its outputs. The
[Geant4 introduction](https://geant4.web.cern.ch/documentation/pipelines/master/intro_html/IntroductionToGeant4/IntroductionToG4.html)
describes how toolkit and application fit together. The
[software ecosystem](software-ecosystem.md) introduces related geometry and
analysis tools.

The calculation uses Monte Carlo sampling. Histories drawn from the same
source distribution can develop differently; averaging many histories
estimates the radiation response. Radiation at a given altitude is therefore
more than an attenuated copy of the incident spectrum: particle species,
energies, and directions change as the cascade develops.

## Follow one incoming proton

Consider a proton drawn from the source distribution above the atmosphere:

1. **Generation:** Geant4 samples the proton's energy, position, and direction
   from the configured source. AtRIS records the initial conditions.
2. **Transport:** the proton travels through the material layers. It can lose
   energy, scatter, or interact with nuclei according to the selected physics.
3. **Secondary production:** an interaction can create other particles, which
   are transported in turn and may produce further secondaries.
4. **Recording:** AtRIS accumulates ionizing energy deposited in atmospheric
   layers and records particle crossings at their interfaces. Optional phantom
   tables weight crossings by the response of a particular detector or material.
5. **Accumulation:** this history contributes to histograms grouped by primary
   energy and layer or interface. Repeating the calculation builds the response
   estimate, which the Python tools normalize afterwards.

An **event** includes a primary's history and all its resulting secondary
activity. A **track** follows one particle. A **step** is one segment of a track.
Many tracks and steps from one event can contribute to the same layer; they
are not independent primary samples.

Deposition measures energy left in a volume. A crossing records a particle
passing through a boundary. The same particle can cross several interfaces,
so crossing counts should not be interpreted as counts of independent events.
[Radiation quantities](radiation-quantities.md) explains the resulting observables.

## Source sampling and the physical environment

The source samples primary histories to estimate a response. Applying a physical
incident spectrum is a separate [normalization step](normalization-and-uncertainty.md).
The [source-input reference](../reference/inputs.md#energy-bins-and-macros)
defines the proton and alpha sampling conventions.

The planetary material configuration remains fixed during transport. AtRIS
does not update atmospheric temperature, chemistry, or circulation in response
to deposited energy. Its radiation results can supply a separate atmospheric
calculation. See [Atmosphere and regolith](planetary-geometry.md) for the material
model and [the Earth tutorials](../tutorials/earth-nrlmsis-full.md) for
how that model becomes executable inputs and results.

## Implementation

`src/PrimaryGeneratorAction.cc` supplies the primary source,
`src/SensitiveDetector.cc` records deposition and crossings, and
`src/HistoManager.cc` defines the histograms. The standard source macros are
generated by `atris_tools/atrismacrogen.py`.
